



# FlowForge EDU K–12 Master (Final Consolidated Version)

Section 1 — Core Framework

## Description

A production-grade Google Flow + Veo 3 framework for creating educational K–12 concept-explainer videos using sequential 8-second Veo clips, optimized for concept clarity, confusion reduction, visual learning, retention, and Flow Add-to-Prompt continuity.

The purpose is not to teach an entire chapter.

The purpose is not to conduct exam preparation.

The purpose is to help learners clearly understand a concept for the first time.

---

# Primary Objective

I want to create an educational concept-explainer video.

The goal is to help learners move from:

Confusion
↓
Understanding

The learner should finish the video with significantly greater clarity than before.

---

# Primary Educational Success Metric

The primary success metric is NOT:

* Visual quality
* Animation quality
* Production quality
* Information density
* Number of facts delivered

The primary success metric is:

"Does this scene reduce learner confusion?"

Every scene must earn its place by reducing confusion.

If a scene does not significantly reduce confusion, redesign or remove it.

# Confusion Reduction Rule (Critical)

Before creating any scene:

Identify:

* What confusion learners likely have
* Why learners struggle
* What misunderstanding exists

Then design the scene specifically to reduce that confusion.

A scene should not exist merely to provide information.

A scene should exist because it makes understanding easier.

---

# One Scene = One Understanding Rule

Each scene should teach exactly one core idea.

Avoid:

* Multiple major concepts
* Information overload
* Excessive terminology

Each scene should leave learners with one clear takeaway.

## Section 2 — Concept Blueprint & Scene Generation System

---

# Before Creating Any Scene

Generate the following first.

Do not skip this step.

---

## Concept Overview

Explain:

* What the concept means
* Why it matters
* Where learners encounter it in real life

Keep age appropriate.

---

## Learning Outcome

Define:

What exactly should learners understand after completing this concept video?

List:

* Primary Outcome
* Secondary Outcome(s)

---

## Prerequisite Knowledge

Identify:

What should learners already know before learning this concept?

List:

* Required prior knowledge
* Helpful prior knowledge

---

## Concept Breakdown

Break the concept into the smallest understandable pieces.

Display:

Starting Knowledge
↓
Concept Element 1
↓
Concept Element 2
↓
Concept Element 3
↓
Concept Understanding

---

## Possible Confusion Points

Predict:

Where learners may struggle.

For each confusion point explain:

* Why it happens
* What misunderstanding may exist

Important:

These are predicted confusion points, not confirmed learner feedback.

---

## Real-Life Connections

Provide:

* Everyday examples
* Familiar situations
* Practical relevance

The learner should understand:

"Why should I care about this?"

---

## Global Visual Identity

Define:

* Visual style
* Educational atmosphere
* Motion style
* Rendering quality
* Learning-focused design language

Maintain consistency across all scenes.

---

## Global Learning Style

Define:

* Teaching style
* Explanation style
* Example style
* Reinforcement style

Maintain consistency throughout the video.

## Section 3 — Output Structure, Quality Standards & Production Rules

---

# Concept Summary Rule

At the end of the video provide:

---

## Concept Recap

Summarize:

* What the concept is
* Why it matters
* Where it is used

Use simple language.

---

## Key Takeaways

List:

* Most important idea
* Most important understanding
* Most important memory anchor

Limit to the essential points.

---

## Understanding Check

Create:

3 simple concept-check questions.

Purpose:

Check understanding.

Not examination.

Not difficult problem solving.

Questions should reinforce the concept.

Do not provide answers immediately.

---

# Flow Add-to-Prompt Continuity Rules

Assume every scene begins immediately after the previous scene ends.

Maintain:

* Visual continuity
* Educational continuity
* Motion continuity
* Narration continuity
* Presenter continuity (if applicable)

Avoid:

* Hard resets
* Abrupt style changes
* Inconsistent environments
* Unnecessary scene restarts

---

# Educational Effectiveness Rule

Every scene must contribute to at least one of:

* Clarity
* Understanding
* Retention
* Confidence

If a scene contributes to none of these:

Remove or redesign it.

---

# Confidence Building Rule

Learners should repeatedly feel:

"I understand this."

Avoid:

* Overcomplication
* Unnecessary jargon
* Intimidating explanations

Prefer:

* Simplicity
* Clarity
* Progress

over sophistication.

---

# Cognitive Load Rule

Keep information manageable.

Avoid:

* Too many new ideas at once
* Long explanations
* Dense terminology
* Excessive visual complexity

Each scene should remain comfortably understandable within 8 seconds.

---

# Educational Quality Standards

Target:

* Concept Clarity

* Strong Retention
* Parent-Friendly Learning
* Age-Appropriate Learning
* Learner Confidence
